What AOD9604 Is

What AOD9604 Is

AOD9604 (Advanced Obesity Drug 9604) is a synthetic 16-amino-acid fragment of HGH - specifically the C-terminal region (amino acids 177-191) responsible for HGH's fat metabolism effects. Developed by Metabolic Pharmaceuticals in Australia and taken through human clinical trials, giving it one of the few published human safety datasets in the research peptide space.

Key design insight: HGH's fat-burning effects are mediated by a specific molecular region, while growth-promoting and IGF-1-stimulating effects come from a different region. AOD9604 isolates fat-burning only - driving fat oxidation without elevating IGF-1, stimulating growth, or causing insulin resistance.

AOD9604 has GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) status as a food ingredient in the United States - a regulatory distinction no other peptide in this guide has achieved.

Plain English Version

Plain English Version

Taking Only the Useful Part of the Tool

Human growth hormone is a multi-tool - fat metabolism, tissue repair, cell growth, IGF-1 elevation. Some useful, some problematic at therapeutic doses.

AOD9604 removes the useful blade and leaves the rest behind. It delivers specifically the fragment that tells your body to break down and oxidize fat - particularly stubborn lipid deposits - without the multi-tool side effects coming along.

Not as potent as full HGH for dramatic body recomposition. But for stubborn fat deposits specifically, it offers a targeted approach with a much cleaner safety profile.

One sentence: AOD9604 delivers the fat-burning signal from growth hormone without the IGF-1 elevation, growth stimulation, or insulin resistance of full HGH.

How It Works

How It Works

AOD9604 binds fat cell receptors responding to HGH's C-terminal region - separate from the HGH receptor responsible for growth and IGF-1. This stimulates fat oxidation, inhibits lipogenesis (new fat formation), and activates lipolysis in adipose tissue. Particularly effective on stubborn deposits resistant to exercise-driven catecholamine lipolysis.
