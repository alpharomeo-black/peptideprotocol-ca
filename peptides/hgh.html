What HGH Is

What HGH Is

HGH (human growth hormone, generically somatropin) is a recombinant version of the 191-amino-acid GH protein produced by the human anterior pituitary. Unlike GH secretagogues (CJC-1295, Ipamorelin, Sermorelin) which stimulate the pituitary to release its own GH, HGH bypasses the pituitary entirely - delivering exogenous growth hormone directly into circulation.

HGH is FDA-approved for several specific indications: childhood GH deficiency, adult GH deficiency, HIV-associated wasting, short bowel syndrome, and Prader-Willi syndrome. Use outside these approved indications - the most common scenario in anti-aging and body composition contexts - is off-label and often illegal for prescribing, making pharmaceutical HGH difficult to access legitimately for wellness purposes.

The critical distinction from GH secretagogues: HGH produces pharmacological (supraphysiological) GH levels, while secretagogues produce physiological GH levels by amplifying the body's own output. This distinction drives the difference in both effectiveness and side effect profile. HGH is more powerful. It is also more complex to dose, requires closer monitoring, and has a more significant side effect profile. The research peptide market offers synthetic recombinant HGH; quality and purity vary significantly and source verification is more important here than for most other research peptides.

Plain English Version

Plain English Version

Delivering the Product vs Ordering More From the Factory

GH secretagogues like CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin are like placing an order at the factory - they tell your pituitary to produce and release its own growth hormone. The factory decides how much to produce based on its current capacity and the signal strength. The output stays within physiological range because the factory has natural governors.

HGH is like receiving a direct delivery. You are not asking the factory to produce anything. You are injecting the product directly. This removes the physiological governors - the dose you inject is the dose that reaches circulation, regardless of what your pituitary might naturally limit.

For people with genuine GH deficiency, that direct delivery is exactly what is needed. For healthy adults trying to optimize body composition, the directness is a double-edged advantage. More GH reaches the system, producing more significant body composition changes. But without the natural regulators, overdosing risk is real, and the side effects of excess GH - water retention, insulin resistance, joint pain, and theoretical long-term risks - require careful monitoring.

One sentence: HGH delivers growth hormone directly into your bloodstream at pharmacological levels, bypassing the pituitary entirely - more powerful than secretagogues, more complex to dose, and more demanding in terms of monitoring.

How It Works

How It Works

Synthetic recombinant HGH (somatropin) has the identical 191-amino-acid sequence as endogenous pituitary GH. When injected subcutaneously it enters circulation directly, binding GH receptors in the liver to stimulate IGF-1 production, in adipose tissue to drive lipolysis, and in muscle to promote protein synthesis. The pharmacological levels achievable with exogenous HGH exceed what secretagogue protocols can produce, explaining the more pronounced body composition effects and the more significant side effect profile.

Common Mistakes

Common Mistakes

Starting at high doses

side effects including water retention, carpal tunnel, and insulin resistance are dose-dependent; start at 1 IU

Not monitoring bloodwork

IGF-1 and fasting glucose need regular monitoring; HGH affects insulin sensitivity

Confusing IU and mg

HGH is dosed in IU, not mg; drawing the wrong unit is a dosing error

Injecting HGH at peak insulin times

GH and insulin are antagonistic; time HGH when insulin is lowest (fasted or between meals)

Freezing reconstituted vials

cold but not frozen; freeze-thaw destroys HGH

Using unverified sources

HGH is a complex protein with more counterfeiting risk than small peptides; source verification is critical

Contraindications And Cautions

Contraindications And Cautions

Active cancer

HGH is absolutely contraindicated with active malignancy; GH and IGF-1 promote cell growth

Cancer history

significant caution; discuss with oncologist; this is a more serious concern than with secretagogues

Diabetic patients

HGH causes insulin resistance; blood glucose monitoring is mandatory

Carpal tunnel syndrome

GH commonly causes fluid retention that compresses the carpal tunnel; existing CTS may worsen

Pituitary disorders
Competitive athletes

WADA prohibited; one of the most tested substances in elite sport

Pregnancy or breastfeeding
Children with closed growth plates

can cause disproportionate growth
