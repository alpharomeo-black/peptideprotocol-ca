Protocol Primer

The Simplest Way To Reduce Dosing Mistakes

The simplest way to reduce dosing mistakes is to stop treating reconstitution like a magic trick.

A vial has a fixed amount of compound in it. BAC water changes the concentration. Concentration changes how much volume you need to draw. The syringe only translates that volume into units. Once those relationships are clear, the calculator becomes a tool for confirmation rather than a black box.

That is why we keep returning to the same fundamentals: how many milligrams are in the vial, how many millilitres of BAC water were added, how much peptide is being targeted for each dose, and what syringe scale is being used to measure the draw. If any one of those values changes, the units change too. People get into trouble when they remember the units but forget the setup that produced them.

A clean protocol record is usually more important than a perfect memory. Write down the peptide name, vial strength, BAC water amount, intended dose, actual units pulled, and the day the vial was mixed. That small habit makes re-checking math much easier, especially when a routine changes or a fresh vial comes in at a different strength than the last one.

Core Formula

The Three Numbers That Drive Everything

1. Vial size in milligrams. This is the total amount of compound in the vial before anything is added. A 15mg vial of retatrutide contains 15mg of retatrutide. Nothing changes that number except how you use it.

2. BAC water volume in millilitres. This is what you control. Add less and the concentration is higher. Add more and the concentration is lower. The goal is to choose a volume that makes the target dose land on a clean unit number.

3. Target dose in milligrams. This is what you are trying to inject. Once you know this, the calculator tells you exactly how many units to draw.

Concentration (mg/mL) = Vial size ÷ BAC water volume Units needed = (Target dose ÷ Concentration) × 100
Practical Example

Choosing A BAC Water Volume That Makes Sense

The goal is to pick a BAC water amount that makes your target dose fall on a round unit number. This reduces measurement error and makes the routine easier to repeat.

Retatrutide 15mg vial 1mL BAC water = 15mg/mL 2.5mg dose = 16.7 units 3mg dose = 20 units 2mL BAC water = 7.5mg/mL 2.5mg dose = 33.3 units 3mg dose = 40 units 3mL BAC water = 5mg/mL 2.5mg dose = 50 units 3mg dose = 60 units

At 3mL BAC water, a 2.5mg dose is exactly 50 units and a 3mg dose is exactly 60 units. Clean numbers are easier to draw, easier to repeat, and easier to verify when you come back to the vial next week.

Recheck Points

When To Recalculate

  • You open a new vial.
  • You change the BAC water amount.
  • You change the target dose.
  • You switch to a different vial size.

Do not assume the units stay the same. Verify the concentration first. One step, every time.

Protocol Checklist
  • Confirm the peptide name and vial strength.
  • Record the exact BAC water volume used.
  • Write down the resulting concentration in mg/mL.
  • Match the target dose to the correct syringe scale.
  • Label the vial with mix date and use-by date.
  • Save the protocol summary before the first draw.
Common Mistake

People often memorize a unit draw without understanding why it works. That becomes a problem the moment the vial size or BAC water amount changes.
