MOTS-c

What MOTS-c Is And How People Commonly Think About It

You know how a car engine can be technically working but running inefficiently? Everything moves. Nothing is broken. But the fuel economy is off. It takes longer to get up to speed. The engine runs hotter than it should. You are burning more gas to go the same distance you used to cover easily. Your metabolism works the same way. By your 40s, especially with insulin resistance, hormonal shifts, or chronic stress, your metabolic engine is running but not efficiently. You eat reasonably, you move, but the results do not match the effort. The engine is not broken. It is just not tuned. MOTS-c is a peptide your own mitochondria produce. Mitochondria are the engines inside your cells, literally every cell in your body has them. MOTS-c is the signal those engines send when they are running well and want the rest of the body to run well too. The problem is that MOTS-c production declines with age, stress, and metabolic dysfunction. When you supplement MOTS-c, you are essentially sending your cells a tune-up signal. It activates a pathway called AMPK, which is your body's fuel efficiency manager. AMPK tells cells to use insulin better, burn fat for energy more readily, and stop storing what should be burned. For people with PCOS or insulin resistance, conditions where the fuel efficiency is chronically poor, MOTS-c is particularly relevant because it addresses the root cause of the inefficiency rather than managing symptoms.

How It Works

How It Works

MOTS-c activates the AMPK pathway, a master metabolic regulator that improves insulin sensitivity, promotes fat oxidation, and enhances mitochondrial function. It also upregulates the folate cycle and one-carbon metabolism, pathways involved in cellular repair and stress response.

The AMPK activation is particularly important for people with insulin resistance (common in PCOS and obesity), MOTS-c improves cellular response to insulin through a mechanism completely separate from GLP-1 class compounds like retatrutide. Combining the two addresses insulin sensitivity from two different angles simultaneously.

Documented Benefits

Documented Benefits

  • Improved cellular energy production and mitochondrial function
  • Enhanced insulin sensitivity, particularly relevant for PCOS and metabolic syndrome
  • Subcutaneous fat reduction (fat under the skin)
  • Improved exercise tolerance and endurance
  • Anti-aging cellular effects
  • Supports NAD+ utilization, MOTS-c increases cellular demand for NAD+, making injectable NAD+ more effective
  • Potential benefits for Hashimoto's thyroiditis through metabolic pathway improvement
  • Reduced metabolic fatigue
Important Distinction

MOTS-c primarily targets subcutaneous fat. Retatrutide's glucagon component targets visceral fat (organ fat). Together they address both fat compartments through completely different mechanisms, this is why they stack well.

CYCLE PROTOCOL

Recommended: 4-8 weeks on, 4 weeks off Reason: MOTS-c stimulates cellular proliferation and mitochondrial biogenesis, cycling reduces the theoretical risk of excessive cellular stimulation

CANCER HISTORY CAUTION

MOTS-c stimulates mitochondrial activity and cellular proliferation. People with personal cancer history or family history of colorectal cancer should obtain medical clearance (including colonoscopy) before using MOTS-c. This is not a prohibition, it is a precautionary step that allows informed use.

Protocol Checklist

Protocol Checklist

  • Confirm vial: MS40 (40mg) or MS10 (10mg)
  • Reconstitute: add 2ml BAC water
  • Concentration: 20mg/ml (MS40) or 5mg/ml (MS10)
  • Standard dose: 10mg = 50 units (MS40 at 20mg/ml)
  • Frequency: 3x weekly (Mon/Wed/Fri)
  • Administer in the morning
  • Cycle: 4-8 weeks on, 4 weeks off
  • Obtain colonoscopy clearance if cancer history applies
  • Track fasting glucose before and during cycle
